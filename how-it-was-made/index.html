How this sheet was made.

The type on this sheet is live text, the engraving at the top of the front page is drawn by your browser as you watch, and the only pictures are the screenshots of the work. This is how the rest is done.

The engraving

The band at the top of the front page is a small 3D scene (perfume bottles, a delivery van, a beach bar) drawn as a line engraving: tone becomes parallel lines, crossed where the shadow is deep. Move the pointer over it and the wet ink smears, the black plate slipping off the red. It is WebGL, drawn with three.js and cut again thirty times a second. Its sun stands where the sun stands over Albania, and its sky takes Tirana’s weather from MET Norway: the clouds gather, rain falls in slanting strokes, snow in white flakes, fog fades the distance and lightning is cut over the sea.

The type

Two faces, both served from this site: Anybody, a grotesque whose width can be set anywhere from 50 to 150, for the headlines, the labels and, set wide, the names of the work; and Inter, a face drawn for screens, for everything that is read. Every headline is fitted line by line, the way a compositor fills a measure: the width and the size of each line are chosen to fill it, and nothing is stretched.

The ink

Headlines arrive as outlines and take ink as a roller passes over them with your scroll. The roller is a 3D object; the ink is the text itself, shown through a window that slides open behind it, so inking a headline moves layers and repaints nothing. Scroll back, and the roller rolls back.

Two languages

Every word is set in English and in Albanian, and each language has its own address. Some browsers come without Albanian month names, so the dateline sets them by hand; the long words of the verse on the front page are hyphenated by hand too, in both languages.

One clock

Everything that moves (the scroll, the ink, the running line, the game, the 3D) is driven by one clock, once a frame. Nothing else keeps its own time, so nothing fights over the same frame.

Speed

Measured on a computer with 2013 integrated graphics, and on a phone-sized screen with the processor slowed four times: while the sheet scrolls, 95 frames in 100 are drawn within about 17 ms, a sixtieth of a second. Getting there meant handing the paper textures to the browser as files instead of text, reading the page before writing to it on every frame, and printing the red sheet once, unseen, while the proof counter runs, so its first appearance costs nothing.

What it runs on

Vite builds it, three.js draws the 3D, GSAP keeps the one clock and Lenis smooths the scroll. No framework, no tracking, no cookies: static files that any host can serve.
